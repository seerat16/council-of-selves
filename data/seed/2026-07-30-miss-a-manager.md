Admitting something out loud that fall-me would have found humiliating: I miss having a manager who teaches me. Someone above me who's seen more, who can look at a plan and say "you're about to make the mistake I made in 2019, turn left here." I've been founder-and-owner-and-boss for so long that I've had no one to learn from, and I'm starting to think that's a poverty, not a freedom. Which is funny, because I once wrote — with total conviction — that big companies are where ambition goes to die [2025-10-12]. Younger me was protecting a wound. What I actually rejected wasn't structure, it was being unseen and overworked. A good manager who teaches you is the opposite of that. I'd take one now. Rated a 4.
